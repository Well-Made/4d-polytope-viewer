4D Polytope Viewer

Interactive stereo viewer for 4D polytopes: the tesseract (hypercube), the 5-cell (simplex), the 16-cell and the 24-cell, plus an ordinary cube to start with. Rotate them in all six 4D planes and see the depth as stereo pairs or with a wiggle. Source code and full documentation on GitHub.

LMB drag: rotate (XZ, YZ) · RMB drag: rotate through 4D (XW, YW)
RMB + mouse wheel: rotate ZW · LMB + RMB together, drag left/right: rotate XY
Wheel or pinch: zoom · Coloured axes in the lower-left corner of each image: fixed global axes (+X right, +Y up, +Z toward the viewer, +W toward the 4D observer)

Two depths, two cues: the edge colour (blue far, red near) shows one depth axis and the edge thickness (thin far, thick near) shows the other, so Z and W can be told apart. Cell buttons switch the highlighted cells on and off; fill opacity sets how strongly they are tinted. The checkbox hides the edges (and fills) of cells that face away from the 4D observer, like hidden lines of a 3D solid; it has no effect on the flat 3D cube.

Hands-free rotation at a steady speed, so you can watch what changes. Click a plane to cycle off → + → −. Each column is a pair of completely orthogonal planes: switch on both planes of a column for a double rotation, or combine several columns. Dragging with the mouse still works on top. Speed is in degrees per second.

Motion parallax: the viewpoint swings back and forth, so near parts move more than far ones. In variant 1 (3D stereo) the wiggle shows the 4D depth W; in variant 2 (4D stereo) it shows the 3D depth Z. Amplitude is the size of the swing, frequency its speed. The swing can go horizontally, vertically, or around a circle (clockwise or counter-clockwise).

Wiggle direction:

How strong the perspective is. The 3D field of view controls the usual perspective along Z (near things look bigger); the 4D field of view does the same along W. A smaller angle puts the camera farther away and flattens the picture.

Settings of the stereo pairs. Parallel viewing: the left eye looks at the left image; cross-eyed: the opposite. Image spacing is the gap between the two images of a pair, pair spacing the gap between the two variants, and stereo strength how different the two images are (more strength = deeper but harder to fuse).

Viewing method:

How to look at it: relax your eyes so that the left and right image of a pair fuse into one 3D picture (parallel viewing; switch to cross-eyed under “Stereo pair” if you prefer that), or just switch the wiggle on and watch the motion parallax. The edge colour and thickness show the two depths, Z and W (blue/thin far, red/thick near); the first tab chooses which is which.
